    What I build at work

    Most of my time goes to two forward-deployed initiatives: I embed with the teams, learn how they really work, and build the AI tools with them.

    Universal Creative · Finance · Built from scratch

    AI for finance reporting and forecasting

    Finance teams kept a separate, frequently reworked spreadsheet for every project, so monthly reporting and forecasting meant hours of manual consolidation before any analysis could start.

    What I designed

    • Human-in-the-loop AI tools, designed from scratch and built with my developers, that bring project finance data into one adaptable source of truth.
    • Monthly insights that surface the top cost drivers and what changed since last month.
    • Risk and contingency analysis that flags transactions worth a closer look.
    • Forecasting support that keeps the analyst in control of every number.

    How I approached it

    As solution architect, I started from how analysts actually close a month, not from what the model could do, and led the developers who built the tools. Every output stays reviewable and editable, and new methods go through evaluation before they reach the team.

    What changed

    Analysts spend their time on judgment instead of copying numbers between files, and the tools are used by finance teams across the organization.

    Universal Creative · Estimating · Built from scratch

    AI-assisted cost estimating

    Estimating large, one-of-a-kind projects depends on years of expertise and messy, non-standardized inputs. The estimators had seen ambitious technology efforts come and go, and they were rightly skeptical.

    What I designed

    • A suite of purpose-built, human-in-the-loop AI tools designed from scratch, several already in daily use.
    • Reading design drawings to draft project scope.
    • Turning messy, non-standardized scope lists into structured estimates, and tracking how they change over time.
    • Retrieving everything previously paid for any item across historical data.

    How I approached it

    I lead the initiative and its developers. Being an expert in technology doesn't make you an expert in someone else's domain, so we replicated existing workflows first: the tools accept the estimators' own Excel files and return results in the same familiar format. We started with small wins they validated, added automation as trust grew, and built shared infrastructure underneath so the step-by-step approach never capped what we could deliver.

    What changed

    The conversation moved from whether it would work to what else we could automate, and estimating work that used to take days is now dramatically faster.

    In progress · Built from scratch

    Project intelligence: a temporal knowledge graph

    Leadership needs to know what changed on a project and what it affects, but the answer is spread across design, scheduling, estimating, budgeting, and finance systems and a steady stream of project decks.

    What I'm building

    A project-intelligence tool built from scratch on a temporal knowledge-graph architecture similar to Graphiti:

    • Every new deck, schedule, or budget update is ingested as an episode, so each fact traces back to its source.
    • An LLM extracts project entities and the relationships between them into a graph.
    • Facts are never overwritten: when something changes, the old version is closed out with its validity window, so the system knows both what is true now and what was true at any point.
    • Retrieval combines semantic search, keyword search, and graph traversal, so "what changed this month, and what does it affect?" follows the links from a scope change to the budgets and schedules downstream.

    The goal

    One view for senior leadership that shows what changed across a project and how it affects budgets, plans, and schedules, with every answer traceable to the document it came from.
